Understand the electrical system

These three paths serve different purposes and must be kept separate.

1 · 230 V AC supply

Schurter inlet J1 feeds the Mean Well 24 V supply PS1 and the two Omron controllers through their respective CONTROL selectors. Protective earth bonds the enclosure, door and relevant equipment.

2 · 24 V enable and demand

Each Schneider CONTROL selector powers its Finder 13.61 enable circuit. The EAO momentary button toggles that channel's ENABLE. The Omron heat-demand output drives the Weidmüller interface, which controls the Crydom input. ACTIVE indication requires ENABLE and demand together.

3 · External heater DC

Each external DC positive path passes through the Lovato protection device, Finder 62.31 directional contact (terminal 34 to 14), Crydom DC SSR and heater. Heater DC negative returns to its corresponding external supply, separately from the 24 V controls.

Build or rewire the controller

Use these documents in sequence. Each has one distinct purpose.

  1. Start with the drawings. Open the circuit diagrams for mains, enable, demand and external heater power.
  2. Identify the parts. The component register is the definitive source of reference designations, manufacturer models and datasheet links.
  3. Identify terminal connections. The terminal allocation appendix gives the reference arrangement, conductor positions and installation notes.
  4. Connect and inspect. Use the searchable From → Via → To schedule (70 listed routes), then complete the inspection and commissioning requirements in the distribution guide and risk assessment.
Additional electrical detail required: the existing 70-route table does not include the Murata input-meter wiring. The meters have internal self-resetting fuses, but no external meter-input fuses are fitted; Murata specifies external 0.25 A / 300 V time-delay supply-wire fusing. Record this protection gap and assess it before electrical approval. Meter requirements →

Diagnose or repair

Start with what the controller is doing. Select the relevant documentation instead of searching through several duplicated explanations.

Before electrical maintenance: switch off, isolate and prove dead on both the AC supply and each external DC heater source. Allow for residual energy and hot equipment. Unexpected or uncontrolled heating requires the site's approved independent DC isolation procedure, not merely an Omron menu command.
Observed symptomRelevant checks and references
Omron controller has no displayCheck the upstream supply, CONTROL selector and respective AC branch using the mains wiring schedule and device references.
ENABLE button does not latch the channelSee EAO SB1/SB2, Finder 13.61 K1/K2, the gated 24 V net and enabled output in the ENABLE circuit schedule.
ACTIVE is off despite requested heatConfirm intended Omron state, enable state and demand path. Use Weidmüller / SSR demand routes and indicator meanings.
ACTIVE lights but the heater does not heatThe lamp does not prove heater current. Refer to external PSU state, DC protection, Finder 62.31 and Crydom path in the DC heater wiring schedule. Do not bypass protective devices.
Temperature reading is wrong or missingCheck the Type K sensor, connector polarity, Omron error display and approved sensor settings. See Type K parts and Omron guidance.
Unexpected output or restart after power returnsKeep heater DC isolated and use the SOP fault procedure. Investigate Omron RESET and Finder enable power-restoration behavior before returning to service.

For replacement work, match the exact manufacturer part number and verify conductor assignments, protective bonding and functional tests before restarting.
